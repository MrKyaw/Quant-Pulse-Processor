# Benchmarks

Generated by `python -m quantpulse bench --write`. Every table is
reproducible on the machine that runs the command; timings are wall
clock medians and will vary with hardware.

## Environment

- Python: 3.12.8
- NumPy: 2.5.4
- Platform: macOS-26.0-arm64-arm-64bit
- Processor: Apple M3

- Package version: 0.1.0

## Pulse-level control

Three-level transmon (anharmonicity -1.2 rad/ns) driven by a 12 ns Gaussian pi-pulse with a DRAG quadrature. The notes below report Lindblad relaxation at T1 and coherence at T2.

| DRAG beta | Pulse area | P(|1>) | Leakage P(|2>) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00 | 3.1416 | 0.9426 | 1.28e-02 |
| 0.25 | 3.1416 | 0.9826 | 6.53e-03 |
| 0.50 | 3.1416 | 0.9963 | 2.62e-03 |
| 0.75 | 3.1416 | 0.9839 | 6.36e-04 |
| 1.00 | 3.1416 | 0.9470 | 6.13e-05 |
| 1.25 | 3.1416 | 0.8890 | 3.64e-04 |

- T1 decay error at t = T1: 1.11e-16.
- T2 coherence error at t = T2: 2.00e-15.
- DRAG cancels the leading leakage path; the residual grows again once beta over-corrects the pulse.
- Leakage falls by more than two orders of magnitude, while the excited-state population peaks near beta = 0.5.
